Split the agent into a fast talker and a background thinker over one conversation timeline.

- `llm` is replaced by `talker`, plus the optional `thinker`, `instructions`, and `tools`. The talker answers every turn; anything that needs a tool or careful reasoning it hands to the thinker with its one tool, `think`, and keeps talking. The thinker follows the instructions and calls the tools in the background, reading new requests and corrections between its steps. When it finishes with something to say, or fails, the talker is woken at the next moment nobody is speaking to tell the user; text it writes alongside tool calls stays in its own history. Without `thinker`, `tools` run on the talker's model.
- The conversation is an append-only `timeline` of entries instead of `messages`: user speech, what the user heard the agent say (marked when they cut it off), the talker's requests, and the thinker's tool calls, results, findings, and errors. It replaces `messages` on `AgentState`, the machine output, the interruption classifier's input, and the turn detector's `Pause`. Every entry is also emitted as a `timeline:entry` event.
- The LLM contract gains `ctx.tools`, `ctx.toolCall()`, and `ctx.reasoning()`. A tool call may carry `providerOptions`, which go back to the model with it as reasoning does, and an `error` for input that does not parse or match the schema, which talkio answers instead of running the tool; `ctx.messages` starts with a system message carrying talkio's role instructions and the agent's `instructions`. talkio runs the tools and calls the model again with their results. `ctx.say()`, `ctx.interrupt()`, `ctx.isSpeaking()`, `ctx.isSpeculative()`, and `ctx.turnConfirmed()` are removed: `think` calls from a speculative reply wait until the turn commits, so no tool runs for a turn the user did not finish.
- Interrupting the talker no longer cancels the work it started; stopping the agent aborts the thinker and its tools.
- Removed `fillers` (the talker acknowledges in its own words), `silence.prompt` (a silence wakes the talker, which decides what to say), and `maxMessages` (each model sees the newest 100,000 characters or so of the timeline). `timeout.llmMs` is now `timeout.talkerMs`; the new `timeout.thinkerMs` bounds each thinker model call and tool call and is two minutes by default; and `agent:error` and `metrics.errors.bySource` report `"talker"` and `"thinker"` instead of `"llm"`.

Testkit: the fake LLM reports tool calls and records every call's context; the harness and scenarios take `tools` and `instructions` and expose a fake `thinker`; scenarios gain `llmToolCall`, `llmThink`, `thinkerToolCall`, `thinkerComplete`, and `thinkerError`, and lose `llmSay` and `llmInterrupt`; the LLM context probe records `toolCalls` and `reasoning`.
